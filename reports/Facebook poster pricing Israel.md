# Charge 250 shekels a month, plus VAT

**Charge 250 ILS a month + VAT per agent, inside a fair range of 200-300 ILS + VAT.** That is what Israeli agents already pay for the same job: the Israeli tools that post to Facebook groups cost about 160-300 ILS a month, and every one of them quotes its price before VAT. The closest rival, PostPro, is built for real-estate agents and charges 300 ILS + VAT. Your app is a little weaker on one point (the agent's computer has to be on, while PostPro runs in the cloud) and stronger on another (the person who wrote it answers the phone). So 250 sits just under the leader and well above the cheap foreign extensions. For launch, give a 7-day free trial, a founding price of 200 ILS + VAT held for 12 months for your first 3-5 agents, and a yearly option of 2,500 ILS + VAT (12 months for the price of 10). One thing decides how you write the price, and I do not know it: whether you are registered as an osek patur or an osek murshe. That is covered below.

## Israeli agents already pay 160-300 shekels for this

Four Israeli products do this exact job, and their prices bunch together. **PostPro** charges **300 ILS a month + VAT**. It is aimed at real-estate agents, gives the first week free, has no commitment and no setup fee, and supports customers by WhatsApp ([PostPro](https://postpro.co.il/)). **Nexus** charges **289 ILS + VAT** month to month, or **249 ILS + VAT** if the customer signs for 12 months ([Nexus](https://nexus-data.co.il/)). **PostPilot** is the one most like yours, because it is also a Windows desktop app. It charges **160 ILS per Facebook profile a month, before VAT**, gives a free trial and lets customers pay monthly or yearly ([PostPilot](https://postpilot.co.il/)). An agent CRM, BrokerCRM, charges 504 ILS + VAT for its "Facebook plan" against 333 ILS for the plan below it, so it prices the Facebook part at about 170 ILS a month ([BrokerCRM](https://brokercrm.co.il/real-estate-brokerage-software)).

| Tool | Price per month | How it runs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PostPro | 300 ILS + VAT, first week free | Cloud, built for agents, WhatsApp support |
| Nexus | 289 ILS + VAT, or 249 on a yearly deal | Cloud |
| PostPilot | 160 ILS + VAT (1-4 profiles) | Windows desktop app, like yours |
| Foreign Chrome add-ons | about $10-47, or about $6 on a yearly plan | Inside Chrome, email support only |

The foreign tools are much cheaper. MultiGroupPoster costs **$9.99 a month** ([MultiGroupPoster](https://multiplegroupposter.com/facebook-auto-poster/)), and PilotPoster, the dearest, costs **$47 a month** ([PilotPoster](https://www.pilotposter.com/pricing/)). They are in English, give no phone support, and leave the customer to fix things alone. Your agents are not comparing against them. They are comparing against PostPro, which speaks Hebrew and answers on WhatsApp.

Since April 2024 the big scheduling tools (Buffer, Publer and others) can no longer post to groups at all. Meta switched off the door they used ([Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2024/01/23/introducing-facebook-graph-and-marketing-api-v19/)), and now they only send a reminder to the phone ([Buffer help](https://support.buffer.com/articles/connecting-and-publishing-to-your-facebook-group-with-buffer-VevqG0mHZG)). The only tools that still post on their own drive the user's own browser, the way your app does. This keeps the market small, which helps you.

## Why 250 and not 300, and not 160

**Not 300**, because PostPro already owns that price, it got there first, and its cloud keeps posting when the agent's computer is off. Yours needs the laptop on (or asleep with the lid shut while plugged in, as you set it up). An agent choosing between equal prices will pick the cloud.

**Not 160**, because PostPilot's 160 is a self-serve tool with a sliding scale for big offices. You are selling more than that. The fee covers three kinds of work you will keep doing: fixing the app when Facebook changes its pages, answering the agent yourself, and adding features. With only a couple of clients today, a low price leaves you doing that work almost for free. Two agents at 160 ILS bring in 320 ILS a month. Two at 250 bring in 500.

**250 is easy to defend in the agent's own terms.** A deal pays the agent about **2% of the price**: 24,000 ILS on a 1.2M flat, 60,000 ILS on a 3M flat ([Bizportal](https://www.bizportal.co.il/career/news/article/20039772)). A year of your app at 250 ILS is 3,000 ILS, so one extra deal a year pays for it many times. For time saved, the rough industry estimate is about 2 hours to post one listing to 50 groups by hand ([RecurPost](https://recurpost.com/schedule-facebook-posts/how-to-post-to-multiple-groups-on-facebook/)). Nobody has measured this properly, so take it as a guess. Even so, 40-80 groups once or twice a day is hours of clicking that the app takes off their hands. A social media freelancer in Israel costs **800-3,000 ILS a month** ([CK Digital 360](https://ckdigital360.co.il/sushial-media/nihul-social-media-le-esek-israel-2026/)), so 250 ILS reads as cheap help, not as a big bill.

One caution. For a new agent earning 8,000-12,000 ILS a month ([Bizportal](https://www.bizportal.co.il/career/news/article/20039772)), 250 ILS is still a noticeable cost. That is why a free trial and no commitment matter. Every Israeli rival offers both.

## Write the price before VAT, if you are an osek murshe

VAT in Israel has been **18% since 1 January 2025** ([Deloitte Israel](https://www2.deloitte.com/content/dam/Deloitte/il/Documents/tax/tax_alerts/2024/19.2024.pdf)). Every Israeli rival writes its price as **"X ILS + VAT"**, so agents expect it that way ([PostPro](https://postpro.co.il/), [PostPilot](https://postpilot.co.il/)). Agents are almost always registered businesses and claim the VAT back, so for them the real cost is the number before VAT.

What you write depends on how you are registered, and I do not know which it is:

| If you are | Write the price as | The agent pays | You keep |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Osek murshe | 250 ILS + VAT | 295 ILS, and gets 45 back from the tax office | 250 ILS |
| Osek patur | 250 ILS (no VAT is added) | 250 ILS | 250 ILS |

Both cost the agent the same 250 ILS in the end, so the price can stay at 250 either way. An osek patur may earn up to **122,833 ILS a year in 2026** ([Green Invoice](https://www.greeninvoice.co.il/magazine/תקרת-עוסק-פטור/)). At 250 ILS a month that is about 40 agents, so the limit does not matter yet. Check this with your accountant before you put a price in writing.

## Launch terms: a free week, a founding price, and a yearly deal

These terms follow what the Israeli rivals already do, plus one standard way to reward the first customers.

| Term | What to offer | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free trial | 7 days free, you set it up for them | PostPro gives a free week, PostPilot a free trial ([PostPro](https://postpro.co.il/), [PostPilot](https://postpilot.co.il/)) |
| Regular price | 250 ILS + VAT a month, cancel any time | Matches the market's "no commitment" norm |
| Founding price | 200 ILS + VAT, for the first 3-5 agents, held for 12 months | Rewards early agents without locking you in for life |
| Yearly deal | 2,500 ILS + VAT (12 months for the price of 10) | Nexus takes 40 ILS a month off for a yearly deal ([Nexus](https://nexus-data.co.il/)) |
| Setup fee | None | PostPro charges none; your setup wizard makes it quick |

On the founding price, pricing advisers say to hold it for a fixed time, such as the first 12 months, not for ever, and to write down the regular price so the discount is visible ([Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/how-to-choose-the-best-pricing-model-for-early-stage-saas-that-actually-converts)). Keep it to a handful of agents, because one founder's warning is that "five founding customers at a steep discount is great data, but fifty is a pricing mistake" ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/decided-not-to-put-a-public-price-on-my-new-tier-taking-founding-customers-instead-here-s-the-thinking-4d9647bce1)). On the yearly deal, a discount of about 15-20% is the usual range ([Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/how-to-structure-the-perfect-annual-discount-a-guide-for-saas-leaders)). "12 months for 10" is about 17%, easy to say, and pays you up front.

You do not need a licence-key system for a few agents you know personally. If someone stops paying, they stop getting fixes, and the next time Facebook changes its pages the app stops working for them.

## Say plainly what the fee does not cover

The price does not buy account safety, and the agreement should say so in simple Hebrew. Meta's terms allow it to **suspend or permanently disable** an account for serious or repeated breaches ([Meta Terms](https://www.facebook.com/legal/terms?locale=en_US)), and its spam rules cover posting "manually or automatically, at very high frequencies" ([Meta Spam policy](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/)). Rival sellers state openly that they do not promise the account will not be flagged or blocked, and they cap what they owe at the fees paid in the last 12 months ([MultiGroupPoster Terms](https://multiplegroupposter.com/terms)). Your installer already asks the agent to accept an account blocking policy (`packaging/policy.txt`), so the agreement can build on it. Add one line on what "fixes when Facebook changes" means in practice: you fix as fast as you can, and posting may stop until the fix is ready. A lawyer or accountant should check the clauses once. Israeli contract law was not part of this research.

## Conclusion

The bigger question is not the number. It is whether the app can match PostPro on what it does, since the prices are already close. Your real edge is that you are the developer and you pick up the phone. That is worth showing in the trial week, not adding to the price. Two facts are still missing and are worth getting soon: how many hours of fixing Facebook changes cost you each month (your git history shows this), and how much time your current agents really save. If fixes turn out to take a lot of your time, raising new agents to 280-300 ILS + VAT later is easy to defend, because PostPro already sits there.
