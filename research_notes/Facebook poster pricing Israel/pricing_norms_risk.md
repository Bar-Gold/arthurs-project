# Pricing a small single-user B2B desktop subscription (Facebook group poster for Israeli real-estate agents): norms and risk factors

Research date: 2026-11-03. Note on source quality: much of the easily found "SaaS pricing benchmark" content online is from vendor content sites (getmonetizely.com and similar) that quote ProfitWell/Paddle numbers second-hand. Where I could not reach the primary report, this is flagged. Vendor marketing claims about time saved are biased upward and are flagged as such.

## 1. Value-based pricing: time spent posting manually to 40-80 groups, and what share of value a tool can charge

### Takeaway
No independent, measured study of manual Facebook-group posting time was found; the available figures are practitioner and vendor estimates of roughly 2-3 minutes per group, i.e. about 1.5-4 hours per round of 40-80 groups, and vendors claim "close to 3 hours daily". The common value-based rule of thumb is to charge 10-20% of the value created (up to 30-50% for unique, business-critical tools), but this rule also comes from secondary pricing-advice sites, not a primary study.

### Cited Findings
- A search-result summary states that posting manually to 50 Facebook groups "takes about 2 hours of clicking, scrolling, copy-pasting, and tab-switching" (about 2-2.4 minutes per group). I could not confirm which page this came from; the results page listed [RecurPost](https://recurpost.com/schedule-facebook-posts/how-to-post-to-multiple-groups-on-facebook/) and [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-many-facebook-groups-can-you-post-in-each-day/) among the sources. Treat as unverified. — [search results incl. RecurPost](https://recurpost.com/schedule-facebook-posts/how-to-post-to-multiple-groups-on-facebook/)
- Vendor/marketing claim: manual Facebook group posting "used to take close to 3 hours daily switching tabs, copying, and pasting"; users "save 3+ hours daily", "many saving over 15 hours per week" (vendor marketing, biased). — [G2 listing for FB Group Bulk Poster](https://www.g2.com/sellers/fb-group-bulk-poster)
- Lilach Bullock (2026 article) recommends a maximum of "5 to 8 groups per day" for promotional content and "at least 10 to 15 minutes between each post"; she reports posting in 22 groups within 90 minutes with identical copy and getting a 24-hour soft block on group posting before post 17, and an account flagged after 6 groups in under 8 minutes. She also says organic group posting typically yields 2-5 inquiries a month. — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-many-facebook-groups-can-you-post-in-each-day/)
- Reviews of a competing tool (MultiGroupPoster) describe the time saving qualitatively only ("like having a free employee alongside you", "save too much time"); one Israeli reviewer said manual posting got "annoying very fast". No hour figures given. — [Trustpilot, MultiGroupPoster reviews](https://nl-be.trustpilot.com/review/multiplegroupposter.com)
- Value-based pricing rule of thumb: price at 10-20% of customer value created; broader range 10-50%; "10-15% value capture for solutions with many alternatives, 15-30% for differentiated solutions with moderate competition, and 30%+ for highly unique, business-critical solutions." Formula: (revenue increase + cost decrease) x (10% to 50%). (Secondary pricing-advice sites, not primary research.) — [Cheddar blog, SaaS value-based pricing](https://www.getcheddar.com/blog/saas-value-based-pricing/); [Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/value-based-pricing-techniques-for-b2b-saas)

### Inferences
- Rough time model (my arithmetic, using the unverified 2-3 min/group figure): 40-80 groups x 2-3 min = 80-240 minutes per round. Once or twice a day, that is roughly 1.3-8 hours a day of manual work, before any spacing between posts. With the 2-4 minute gap the app already uses, a manual user doing the same pacing would be tied up even longer.
- Illustrative value calculation (the hourly value is an ASSUMPTION, not sourced; the client should supply their own): if an agent values an hour at X shekels and saves H hours a month, value = X x H. Example: 2 hours/day x 22 working days = 44 hours/month. At an assumed 100 NIS/hour that is 4,400 NIS/month of time; 10-20% of that is 440-880 NIS/month. The real number depends heavily on the agent's actual hourly value and whether they would otherwise post at all or pay an assistant.
- The tool has alternatives (cheap self-serve extensions, see section 2), which pushes toward the low end (10-15%) of value capture on software alone; personal support, Hebrew, local service and maintenance are the differentiators that justify going above the self-serve price.
- An alternative anchor for "value" is what a human assistant/virtual assistant would cost to do the posting; no Israeli figure was found (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No independent, measured (timed) study of manual posting to Facebook groups was found. All figures are vendor or practitioner estimates.
- No reliable source found for an Israeli real-estate agent's hourly value or typical income, or the cost of hiring an Israeli assistant for social posting. These should come from the clients themselves.
- No evidence found on how many leads/deals Facebook group posting produces for Israeli agents (Bullock's 2-5 inquiries/month is a general, non-Israeli figure).

## 2. Pricing norms for small SaaS / desktop subscriptions for SMBs

### Takeaway
Direct competitors (self-serve Facebook group posting tools) sit at roughly $9-$47 a month with no personal support. Annual-prepay discounts typically run 10-20% (often cited average 16%, from ProfitWell via a secondary source). For founding customers, the advice is a time-limited discount (e.g. a rate held for 12 months) on a small, capped number of clients, rather than a lifetime price.

### Cited Findings
Competitor price anchors
- PilotPoster (Chrome extension, Facebook group poster): Standard plan $47/month billed monthly, unlimited groups, scheduling, auto-repeat campaigns; no free plan (as of May 2026 per aggregator). — [xpay.sh agent-ready index, PilotPoster](https://www.xpay.sh/agent-ready-index/pilotposter/)
- MultiGroupPoster: a reviewer mentions a "$9/month service" with instant support. — [Trustpilot, MultiGroupPoster reviews](https://nl-be.trustpilot.com/review/multiplegroupposter.com)
- MultiGroupPoster grandfathers current subscribers at existing rates for a minimum of 12 months after a price change announcement. — [MultiGroupPoster Terms](https://multiplegroupposter.com/terms)

Annual vs monthly
- Industry standard annual discount "typically ranges from 10-20%, with 16% being the average according to a ProfitWell analysis of over 6,000 SaaS companies"; sweet spot 15-20%. (Secondary; the ProfitWell/Paddle page I fetched did not show these numbers.) — [Monetizely, annual discount guide](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/how-to-structure-the-perfect-annual-discount-a-guide-for-saas-leaders); the Paddle page fetched carried no figures: [Paddle ProfitWell Report, discounting benchmarks](https://paddle.com/studios/shows/profitwell-report/discounting-benchmarks)
- Claims (secondary, same caveat): 52.7% of SaaS companies offer annual discounts; companies with annual billing see about 30% lower churn; per Paddle's survey, 40% of deals closed with a discount would have closed at full price anyway. — [Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/why-annual-billing-discounts-work-better-than-you-think-a-revenue-game-changer-for-saas)

Founding-customer / early discounts
- Early adopter model: set a transparent regular price, offer "a significant but time-limited discount (e.g., '50% off for 6 months')", and clearly state grandfathering. Hold rates "for a fixed window like the first twelve months rather than for life". — [Monetizely, early-stage pricing](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/how-to-choose-the-best-pricing-model-for-early-stage-saas-that-actually-converts)
- Founding-customer approach: a small handful of early customers with locked-in early rates; "five founding customers at a steep discount is great data, but fifty is a pricing mistake that compounds." — [Indie Hackers post on founding customers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/decided-not-to-put-a-public-price-on-my-new-tier-taking-founding-customers-instead-here-s-the-thinking-4d9647bce1)
- Warnings: heavy discounting is reported to cut customer lifetime value by about 30%; at 80% gross margin a 20% discount removes 25% of gross profit on that deal; a discount "follows the customer for their whole life". (Secondary aggregations.) — [Kinde, early customer pricing](https://kinde.com/learn/billing/pricing/early-customer-pricing-finding-the-balance-between-learning-and-revenue/); [Sixteen Ventures, grandfathered customers](https://sixteenventures.com/grandfathered-customers/)

Refunds / commitment (from a direct competitor)
- MultiGroupPoster: 7-day money-back guarantee on the first paid subscription; after 7 days all payments are final for monthly and annual plans; renewals not refundable; must cancel before renewal. — [MultiGroupPoster Terms](https://multiplegroupposter.com/terms)

### Inferences
- The developer's offer is not a self-serve tool: it includes personal support, Hebrew, and on-call fixes when Facebook changes. That is closer to a "software + managed service" retainer, so pricing well above the $9-$47 self-serve band is defensible, but the self-serve band is what a price-sensitive client will compare against.
- With 2-10 clients, each client is a large share of income; an annual prepay with a 10-20% discount (e.g. "12 months for the price of 10", about 17%) both reduces churn risk and funds maintenance time.
- A one-time setup/installation fee is common in service-like offers, but I found no reputable benchmark for it (see Gaps). For this product the setup (installer, first-run wizard, one manual Facebook login, choosing groups) is a real time cost that could be charged once or waived as the founding-customer incentive.
- Founding-customer pricing fits well: cap it to the first few clients, hold the rate for a fixed period (e.g. 12 months), and write down the regular price so the discount is visible.

### Gaps
- Could not reach primary OpenView, ProfitWell/Paddle, Stripe or a16z reports with numeric SMB price bands in the tool-call budget; the annual-discount numbers above are second-hand.
- No reputable benchmark found for setup fees or minimum-commitment terms for micro-SaaS sold to a handful of clients.
- No Israeli-market price found for Facebook group posting software or services (Hebrew searches returned nothing usable).
- Competitor prices come from an aggregator snippet (PilotPoster) and one review (MultiGroupPoster), not the vendors' own pricing pages.

## 3. Risk factors: Facebook Terms, account bans, maintenance, and what similar sellers disclose

### Takeaway
Meta's Terms let it suspend or permanently disable accounts for clear, serious or repeated breaches, ban using the products to send spam, and ban automated data access/collection without permission; its Spam policy explicitly covers posting "manually or automatically, at very high frequencies" and repetitive content. Competitors deal with this by stating plainly that no account safety is guaranteed, that the user bears the risk, and by capping liability at fees paid. The ban risk falls on the client's own account, which argues for clear written disclosure and against promising results, more than for a price change.

### Cited Findings
Meta's official texts
- Terms of Service, last revised 1 January 2025: "You may not access or collect data from our Products using automated means (without our prior permission) or attempt to access data you do not have permission to access." — [Meta Terms of Service](https://www.facebook.com/legal/terms?locale=en_US)
- Same Terms: you may not "use the services to send spam, or do anything else that could disable, overburden, interfere with, or impair the proper working" of the products. — [Meta Terms of Service](https://www.facebook.com/legal/terms?locale=en_US)
- Same Terms: "If we determine, in our discretion, that you have clearly, seriously or repeatedly breached our Terms or Policies, including in particular the Community Standards, we may suspend or permanently disable your access to Meta Company Products". — [Meta Terms of Service](https://www.facebook.com/legal/terms?locale=en_US)
- Community Standards, Spam policy: prohibits "Posting, sharing, engaging with content or creating accounts, Groups, Pages, Events or other assets, either manually or automatically, at very high frequencies", and Meta may also restrict accounts engaging at lower frequencies "when other indicators of Spam (e.g., posting repetitive content) or signals of inauthenticity are present." — [Meta Transparency Center, Spam](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/)
- Note: the automated-means sentence in the Terms is worded about accessing or collecting data; automated posting is reached mainly through the spam clause and the Spam policy above. (My reading of the quoted texts.)

Real-world restriction evidence
- A 24-hour soft block on group posting after 22 groups in 90 minutes with identical copy; an account flagged after 6 groups in under 8 minutes. — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-many-facebook-groups-can-you-post-in-each-day/)

What similar sellers disclose
- MultiGroupPoster Terms: does not guarantee that "Your Facebook account will not be flagged, restricted, or suspended"; states "Facebook may at any time and at their sole discretion suspend, restrict, or terminate accounts" and users assume this risk; "We are not responsible for any consequence imposed on you by Facebook arising from your use"; users responsible for account safety, pacing settings and legal compliance; liability capped at amounts paid in the preceding 12 months; excludes indirect damages "like lost accounts, revenue, or data". — [MultiGroupPoster Terms](https://multiplegroupposter.com/terms)
- A Taiwanese Facebook auto-post tool's download precautions: using the software carries risk; if the account is reported or blocked due to improper use, contact Facebook support; the company is not liable; if you disagree, do not download or use the software. — [BlueEyes download precautions](https://fbuy.blueeyes.tw/download_precautions-en.php)
- A competing tool's 1-star review alleges only 30% posting success, showing that reliability (which degrades when Facebook changes its site) is a live complaint in this category. — [Trustpilot, MultiGroupPoster reviews](https://nl-be.trustpilot.com/review/multiplegroupposter.com)

### Inferences
- Effect on price, maintenance side: the fee is buying ongoing labour (fixing selectors when Facebook changes markup), not just a licence. That is a real, recurring and unpredictable cost to the developer, which supports a recurring fee rather than a one-time sale, and supports a price floor set by the developer's own expected maintenance hours divided across the 2-10 clients.
- Effect on price, ban side: the main loss from a ban falls on the client (their Facebook account and its group memberships). Sellers in this category do not price this risk in as insurance; they disclaim it. Pricing very high could make a ban feel like a bigger betrayal; a moderate price plus honest disclosure is the norm.
- The contract/terms should say it, in plain Hebrew, because competitors do and because the client is non-technical: (1) automating posts is against Facebook's Terms; (2) Facebook can restrict or disable the account at its discretion, and no result or account safety is guaranteed; (3) the client chooses what to post and to which groups; (4) liability capped at fees paid (competitor uses 12 months); (5) what "fixes when Facebook changes" means in practice (best effort, typical response time) and that posting may stop until a fix ships. The project already has an account-blocking policy the installer asks the client to accept (`packaging/policy.txt`), which can be the basis.
- Israeli consumer and contract law specifics (for example whether a liability cap or "no refunds" is enforceable for a business client) were not researched; a short check with an Israeli lawyer or accountant is advisable before relying on any clause.

### Gaps
- No data found on actual ban rates for users of Facebook group posting tools.
- No source found on how often Facebook changes the group-post markup (maintenance frequency); the developer's own git history is the best evidence for this.
- Did not find the official Meta "Automated Data Collection Terms" text in budget.
- Israeli legal requirements for B2B software terms not researched.

## 4. Selling a desktop .exe on subscription: licence keys, non-payment, trials

### Takeaway
Licensing services such as Keygen support "timed" licences that expire and can be used for trials and subscriptions, plus device-locking and offline validation; grace periods after expiry or while offline are common (examples range from about 7 to 90 days). For 2-10 clients known personally, simple measures are proportionate.

### Cited Findings
- Keygen lists licensing models including perpetual ("one of the most common licensing models for small software apps due to its simplicity"), timed ("expires at some point in time. These can be used for trials, subscriptions, and more"), node-locked ("can only be used on a single activated device at any given time"), floating, feature-based, metered, and offline (cryptographically signed, validated without a connection). — [Keygen, choosing a licensing model](https://keygen.sh/docs/choosing-a-licensing-model/)
- Grace periods: a subscription licence can carry a grace period beyond the expiry date (example: expires 31 December, grace until 14 January); the vendor decides whether and how long. — [Nalpeiron docs, subscription grace periods](https://docs.nalpeiron.com/use-cases/subscription-grace-periods)
- Offline tolerance varies: some desktop apps stay valid 7 days without a connection, others up to 90 days. — [Seequent Oasis montaj, working offline](https://help.seequent.com/Oasismontaj/2025.1/Content/ss/om_reference/common_reference/intro_to_om/working_offline.htm); [Office Watch, Microsoft 365 grace period](https://office-watch.com/2021/check-your-microsoft-365-licence-grace-period/)
- Trial/refund practice in this exact category: MultiGroupPoster gives a 7-day money-back guarantee on the first paid subscription rather than relying only on a free trial. — [MultiGroupPoster Terms](https://multiplegroupposter.com/terms)
- PilotPoster offers no free plan (as of May 2026). — [xpay.sh, PilotPoster](https://www.xpay.sh/agent-ready-index/pilotposter/)

### Inferences
- For this product, non-payment is partly self-enforcing: because Facebook changes its site, a client who stops paying stops getting fixes and new versions, and the tool will eventually break. That alone may be enough for 2-10 clients known personally.
- If a hard stop is wanted, a timed licence key checked at startup (with a grace period of one to two weeks and a clear Hebrew message, never a mid-batch stop) is the standard approach. Any check must not break the "never post twice" and "halt, tell the user" rules, and must not stop a batch mid-way; a licence check at app start or before a new batch is safer.
- A short paid pilot or a money-back window (e.g. 7-14 days) is more common in this category than a long free trial, and fits a service that includes setup work.

### Gaps
- No reputable survey found on trial-length norms for SMB desktop software specifically (2023-2026).
- Costs of licensing services (Keygen, Lemon Squeezy, Paddle licence keys) were not checked in budget.
